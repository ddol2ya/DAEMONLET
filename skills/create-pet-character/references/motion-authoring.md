# Expressive motion from independent illustrations

Default to movement that is visible at the intended desktop size. A technically changing vertex that moves less than a pixel on screen is not sufficient acting. Preserve the user's desired personality and motion intensity; a focused working pose can be calmer than a celebratory reaction.

## Plan the action before rigging

For each pose and each click variant, record a distinct intent, the moving body parts, fixed contacts and the main action beat. Examples: weight shift while waiting; downward focus and a small thinking nod while working; recoil and recovery on failure; acknowledging wave on cancellation; gaze followed by head/body turn when disconnected; fidget during boredom; energetic lift and settle on happiness; leaning into a petting gesture. Variants should differ in gesture/timing, not just a seed or phase offset.

Use a small preparation in the opposite direction, a clear main action, a restrained overshoot and a settling tail. Let gaze lead the head, head/shoulders lead the body or arm where appropriate, and hair/ribbons follow with a delay. Avoid moving every part with the same sine wave. Loops must join continuously; a once-only reaction settles and holds instead of snapping back. Entry/exit, rapid repeated clicks, task preemption and re-entry need review.

## Source-specific rig

- **Inspect layer contents before authoring local movement.** A decomposition label such as `handwear` is not proof that the layer contains only a movable arm. Inspect each isolated layer against the composite and mark its intended moving parts, attached roots and unrelated fixed content. If shoulder/torso, hair, skin or another object that must move differently is baked into it, the layer is not ready. Split it into independently controlled parts and reconstruct the paint that movement reveals; re-decompose or remake that part when a clean split is impossible. Preserve the selected source and work in a new round. Do not pass a mixed layer by reducing amplitude, hiding the seam behind hair, or declaring a single pivot pinned.
- Review the repaired split in neutral and over the intended motion range before adding acting. Confirm no missing backing, duplicated paint/outline, transparent holes, order error or stray fixed pixels. Pieces meeting at a joint need shared deformation near the attachment plus reviewed overlap/underpaint. Splitting textures alone does not keep the joint connected. A tapered influence is appropriate for an already valid connected part; it is not a substitute for fixing mixed semantic content.
- Measure shoulder/elbow/wrist and hair/accessory roots from the selected illustration. Use separate movable regions, joint-local origins and tapered influence so a sleeve root remains attached while its cuff moves. Do not rotate a whole painted sleeve around the canvas center.
- A hand touching the cheek, hair, another hand or a prop needs its own contact constraints. Use existing `headFollow`/deformation sources where appropriate; a hand attached to the head must not also receive an unrelated arm sway. Capture its contact landmark at motion peaks and during transitions.
- Plan enough source coverage for the intended motion. Large turns, newly exposed surfaces, crossed limbs or substantial perspective changes may need another complete illustration or reconstructed hidden paint. Do not stretch a single flat source until the face/body distorts, or add unrequested pose variants to hide that limit.
- Secondary hair/ribbon motion uses anchored roots and smaller delayed movement. It is not a reason to increase all physics or body-shape parameters. Recheck transparent outlines and occlusion as locks move apart.

## Reusable authoring command

`creator.mjs motion --source <run> --pose <id> --round <fresh-round> --plan <motion-plan.json>` copies the selected model into a fresh sibling round, writes a source-local `pose.json` and `motion-authorship.json`, and reports the path. It does **not** silently select or approve the candidate. Update `models.json` after inspecting it, then capture/review. Existing source artwork and packs remain preserved.

Plan fields:

- `intent`: pose-specific acting description.
- `gesture`: `breathe`, `focus`, `nod`, `recoil`, `celebrate`, `wave`, `look`, or `pet`. These are timing starting points, not final performance guarantees.
- `strength`: 0.5–1.5, default 1; tune after measuring display-size travel. Do not repeatedly boost it to compensate for a wrong pivot or pinned mesh.
- `joints`: optional measured local motions. Each has the effective runtime layer name and bounds from capture diagnostics, `origin: {x,y}` in that layer's normalized coordinates, `influence: {axisX,axisY,start,end}`, optional `rotationDeg`/`translateX`/`translateY`, and `lag` (0–0.15 of a cycle, default .06). Runtime names can differ from PNG names, e.g. a pre-split hand becomes `handwear_1`; the tool loads the actual rig to validate names and records joint frames. Rotation/translation values are candidate maxima before strength. The tool rejects independent motion on `contactLocked` joints. Hand contact needs rig constraints and a target landmark instead.
- Each joint also needs `layerReview: {status: "ready", movingParts: ["..."], remainingFixedParts: [], evidence: ["qa/isolated-layer.png"]}` after actual isolated-layer inspection. Evidence paths are relative to the source run. Unresolved mixed content or missing evidence blocks authoring. This declaration records human/model inspection; it is not automatic pixel classification.
- `landmarks`: measured points, not copied coordinates. Each has `name`, effective runtime `layer`, `tip: [x,y]`, `root: [x,y]`, optional `targetLayer`/`targetPoint` for contact, and optional `minTravelAt320px` for the intended visible action. Use actual canvas coordinates inside the corresponding meshes. Travel is separate from attachment safety.
- `connections`: every independently moving layer needs attachment coverage. Each connection has a distinct kebab-case `name`, `layer`, `parentLayer`, at least three measured `points: [{child: [x,y], parent: [x,y]}, ...]` spanning the **whole** attachment, a source-pixel `maxDriftPx`, and `reviewBounds: [x,y,width,height]` containing the seam and enough surroundings to see gaps. Cover both ends and intermediate bends of the seam; multiple connections may be needed. Choose a tolerance that keeps the source contour intact, never enlarge it to excuse visible separation. Points cannot lie outside their actual meshes. A single stable root does not establish coverage.

Without joints, the command authors head/body/gaze motion only. To obtain a wave or arm gesture, author the corresponding free limb joints; the gesture name cannot create missing rig regions. Add/adjust full motion tracks in the candidate `pose.json` when the timing starter does not fit the action. A body illustration that is already waving still needs motion in the free hand if the intended action is an animated wave.

## Motion acceptance

Capture `--motion --contacts` and inspect the actual 320/460px playback, plus 1280px action peaks. Set a pose-appropriate screen-space travel target on at least one main moving landmark; a few pixels may fit focused breathing, while a clear wave or celebratory action usually needs more. These are acting choices, not universal anatomy thresholds. Document intentional small movement rather than automatically passing nearly static output.

The capture tool measures every 30fps sample, exact motion keyframe times and loop closure. It rejects missing attachment coverage, mixed/unreviewed local-motion layers, excessive change in child/parent boundary separation, lost hand contact or an unmet configured travel target. Authorship, tolerances and isolated-layer evidence are fingerprinted with the assets. Failed measurements still produce enlarged transparent connection crops for every sample in the gallery; scrub/play them on light and dark backgrounds, especially the reported worst times. A failed candidate is never accepted because another metric passed.

Numeric travel can be produced by a detached limb, and a few well-chosen points can miss a hole between them. Inspect the full shoulder/wrist seam, finger contact, neckline, overlapping clothes and hair gaps, plus source identity and final eye/mouth review after rig changes. If an existing false pass prompted the repair, demonstrate that the old candidate fails the new regression check before reviewing the replacement. A code/test fix is not a repaired character until the new visual candidate has passed inspection.
